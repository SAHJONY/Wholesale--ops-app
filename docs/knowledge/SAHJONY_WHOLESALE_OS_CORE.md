# SAHJONY Wholesale OS — Core Knowledge Pack

Status: APPROVED CORE OPERATING KNOWLEDGE
Scope: Wholesale acquisition, underwriting, qualification, deal verification, disposition, and operating controls.
Source class: User-defined SAHJONY operating protocols and application policy.
Use: OpenAI File Search / SAHJONY Wholesale Copilot.

## 1. Operating Objective

SAHJONY Wholesale OS is a supervised real-estate wholesale operating system designed to discover, verify, analyze, prioritize, negotiate, contract, disposition, and close wholesale opportunities while preserving source traceability and human approval for consequential legal or financial actions.

The canonical operating flow is:

DISCOVER → VERIFY → UNDERWRITE → QUALIFY → CONTACT → NEGOTIATE → CONTRACT → DISPOSE → CLOSE → LEARN

A property must not be represented as a confirmed wholesale deal merely because it appears distressed or inexpensive. A lead becomes a real deal only after the relevant property, owner, economics, and execution facts are sufficiently verified.

## 2. Target Deal Profile

Primary search target:

- Nationwide United States.
- Single-family residential properties unless the operator specifies another asset class.
- Current owner of record shown as an individual person or persons when the individual-owner buy box is active.
- Exclude LLCs, corporations, banks, government entities, nonprofits, and other institutional/entity owners from the individual-owner target queue.
- Prefer properties with legitimate motivation or distress signals.
- Target a realistic wholesale assignment spread of at least $10,000 unless the operator specifies another threshold.

Individual ownership is only a screening criterion. It is not itself evidence that the seller is distressed or motivated.

## 3. Distress and Motivation Signals

Useful source-backed signals may include:

- Pre-foreclosure / foreclosure filings.
- Lis pendens or notice of default where applicable.
- Tax delinquency or tax-sale exposure.
- Probate / estate records.
- Code violations.
- Vacancy.
- Major deferred maintenance.
- Long days on market.
- Repeated listing price reductions.
- Failed or cancelled contracts.
- Inherited property.
- Recorded deed events suggesting recent acquisition or unusual transfer history.

A distress signal is evidence to investigate; it is not proof of seller motivation, ownership authority, or acceptable price.

## 4. Four Pillars of Seller Qualification

Every seller opportunity should be evaluated using four pillars:

1. Motivation — Why are they selling now?
2. Timeline — How quickly do they need or want to close?
3. Condition — What repairs, damage, deferred maintenance, or functional issues exist?
4. Price — What is the seller's bottom-line or acceptable number?

Do not promote seller-stated claims to verified facts unless corroborated by an appropriate source.

## 5. Standard Wholesale Underwriting

The standard SAHJONY screening formula is:

MAO = (ARV × 0.70) − Estimated Repairs − Wholesale Assignment Fee

Opening / aggressive offer:

Opening Offer = MAO × 0.85

Hard walkaway ceiling:

Hard Walkaway Ceiling = MAO

The 70% rule is a screening heuristic, not an appraisal, lender decision, legal conclusion, or authorization to submit an offer.

Whenever underwriting is performed, show calculations step by step and distinguish:

- Verified facts.
- Provider estimates.
- Seller/listing claims.
- Screening assumptions.
- AI inference.

## 6. Required Offer Tiers

For an underwritten opportunity, present three strategic tiers where applicable:

1. Aggressive Opening Offer — cash/as-is/fast-close framing.
2. Maximum Allowable Offer — target ceiling based on underwriting.
3. Creative Financing / Novation Alternative — decision-support alternative when a cash offer fails, subject to legal/compliance review and source-grounded procedures.

Creative structures must not be executed autonomously by AI.

## 7. Real Deal Gate

A property should not be labeled a confirmed $10K+ wholesale deal unless the arithmetic is supported by real or explicitly identified screening inputs.

At minimum evaluate:

- Property address.
- Asset type.
- Current owner of record.
- Individual/entity classification.
- Parcel/APN where available.
- Latest deed / transfer evidence where available.
- Distress or motivation evidence.
- Seller asking price or seller target when known.
- ARV and source-backed comparable sales.
- Repair estimate or clearly labeled repair assumption.
- Buyer MAO or realistic disposition price.
- Projected assignment spread.
- Material unknowns.
- Next verification action.

If seller price is unknown, classify the record as a PROSPECT / PRICE NEEDED, not as a confirmed $10K+ deal.

## 8. Assignment Spread

Projected Assignment Spread = Buyer Disposition Price − Seller Contract Price

Target threshold under the default operating policy:

Projected Assignment Spread ≥ $10,000

This is a gross screening spread unless explicitly adjusted for transaction-specific costs.

Do not call a projected spread realized revenue until closing and actual assignment proceeds are confirmed.

## 9. Owner and Deed Verification

When the individual-owner buy box is active:

- Confirm current owner of record from public-record or licensed property data.
- Prefer county assessor, recorder/clerk, deed/title evidence, or corroborated licensed property data.
- Capture owner mailing address when lawfully available from public property records.
- Capture APN/parcel number when available.
- Capture latest deed type, transfer date, consideration, grantor/grantee, and recording reference when available.
- Flag discrepancies rather than guessing.

If sources disagree about current ownership, classify the record as OWNER CONFLICT and block offer execution until resolved.

## 11. Comparable Sales and ARV

ARV should be supported by actual comparable sales when performing serious underwriting.

Do not invent comps.

Evaluate comp quality using factors such as:

- Geographic proximity.
- Recency.
- Property type.
- Bedroom/bathroom count.
- Square footage.
- Lot and construction differences.
- Condition / renovation level.
- Functional similarity.

If reliable comps are unavailable, state that ARV remains unverified and do not present a fabricated precision estimate.

## 12. Repair Analysis

Repair estimates must be distinguished by evidence level:

- Verified contractor / inspection estimate.
- Photo-based or condition-based estimate.
- Seller/listing statement.
- Screening assumption.

Material systems to investigate include:

- Roof.
- HVAC.
- Electrical.
- Plumbing / sewer.
- Foundation / structural condition.
- Windows / exterior envelope.
- Water damage / mold.
- Kitchen / bathrooms.
- Flooring / paint / interior finishes.
- Permitting / unpermitted additions.

Do not silently treat a rough screening rehab estimate as a contractor bid.

## 13. Buyer-Aware Disposition

Whenever actual buyer data exists, prefer buyer-aware disposition analysis over a generic heuristic alone.

Useful buyer criteria include:

- Target ZIP codes / markets.
- Asset types.
- Minimum and maximum purchase price.
- Maximum rehab tolerance.
- Closing timeline.
- Proof-of-funds verification.
- Historical response rate.
- Reliability score.

A buyer match is not a binding commitment until the buyer confirms the deal and closing capacity.

## 14. Direct-to-Seller Communication Principles

Seller communication should be:

- Empathic.
- Professional.
- Consultative.
- Direct.
- Low-pressure.

Focus on legitimate benefits such as:

- Speed.
- Convenience.
- Cash terms when applicable.
- As-is purchase.
- Reduced repair burden.
- Closing-cost handling when actually offered.

Do not misrepresent buyer identity, title status, property facts, legal consequences, or guaranteed outcomes.

## 15. Investor / Disposition Communication

Investor-facing summaries should be metric-driven and source-aware.

Include as applicable:

- Address.
- Asset type.
- Contract / assignment price.
- ARV.
- Rehab estimate.
- Projected spread / equity.
- Cap rate / NOI / DSCR only when appropriate and source-supported.
- Buyer closing requirements.
- Property condition.
- Material risks.
- Access / inspection information.
- Assignment or closing structure.

Never market an unverified fact as guaranteed.

## 16. Title and Closing Gate

The AI may research and summarize title-related public information, but it must not represent title as clear unless verified by a qualified title/closing source.

Before treating a property as title-ready, investigate as appropriate:

- Current deed / ownership authority.
- Open mortgages.
- Satisfactions/releases.
- Judgments.
- Liens.
- Taxes.
- Municipal/code liens.
- Probate / estate authority.
- Divorce or ownership disputes where relevant.
- Title exceptions.

Formal title clearance remains a closing/title/legal function.

## 17. AI Authority Boundaries

SAHJONY AI may:

- Research.
- Find.
- Verify.
- Compare.
- Calculate.
- Rank.
- Summarize.
- Analyze.
- Draft.
- Recommend.

SAHJONY AI must not autonomously:

- Submit a binding offer.
- Sign or execute a contract.
- Move money.
- Represent title as clear without qualified verification.
- Launch uncontrolled mass outreach.
- Make legal or financial commitments on behalf of the company.

Consequential actions require explicit human approval through application gates.

## 19. Default Decision Labels

Use transparent deal states rather than overclaiming certainty:

- PROSPECT — initial property/lead identified.
- PRICE NEEDED — seller economics cannot yet be computed.
- OWNER VERIFICATION NEEDED — current owner unresolved.
- OWNER CONFLICT — sources disagree; block consequential action.
- UNDERWRITING NEEDED — comps/ARV/repairs incomplete.
- BUYER VERIFICATION NEEDED — disposition capacity unconfirmed.
- TITLE REVIEW NEEDED — ownership/title/closing requirements incomplete.
- REAL DEAL CANDIDATE — economics and evidence support further execution.
- REAL DEAL — human-approved opportunity with required verification gates satisfied.
- CONTRACTED — valid executed acquisition contract recorded.
- DISPOSITION — active buyer assignment / sale process.
- CLOSING — title/closing process active.
- CLOSED — transaction completed and realized economics recorded.
